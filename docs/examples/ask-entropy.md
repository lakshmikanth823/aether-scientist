Entropy is a fundamental concept in physics that quantifies the measure of disorder or randomness within a system. It's often referred to as thermal energy in a more intuitive context. The second law of thermodynamics states that the total entropy of an isolated system will always increase over time, meaning that it gets hotter and less organized. This implies that systems tend towards greater disorder and less organization.

For instance, consider a cup of hot coffee on a chilly morning. Initially, the molecules were randomly moving around until they reached room temperature. As heat flows into the coffee from its surroundings, some molecules move faster while others slow down, causing the overall motion to become randomized. Eventually, all molecular movements settle at their equilibrium positions. At this point, the final state has higher entropy because the coffee is no longer randomly disordered; instead, we have a lower-energy configuration with fewer particles than before.

The relationship between entropy and information theory also plays a crucial role in modern science. Entropy can be thought of as the amount of uncertainty associated with a specific event or process, which depends not only on the number of possible outcomes but also on how those outcomes interact. For example, if two events are independent, the entropy increases by one bit for each new fact discovered about both
